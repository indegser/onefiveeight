# Tonal Reasoning from Moises Results

## Key and Function

Use the full available order, not a bag of chord names. A pitch collection shared by relative keys does not select the tonic. Strong recurring arrivals, phrase-final stability, and cadential approaches help distinguish centers. Without phrase/metric evidence, call an interpretation plausible rather than declaring a cadence or a modulation.

Declare the key for every numeral interpretation. In a major-key reading, chromatically altered chord quality and applied function may need two labels, such as major `III` and `V/vi`. An applied-dominant label describes a possible function/tendency; it does not prove that its expected target follows. A chord's appearance alone does not establish a new key.

## Non-Diatonic Chords

- **Secondary dominant:** explain the proposed target and its leading tone, then state whether the observed progression reaches, delays, or avoids that target. Absence of the target may leave a chromatic-chord interpretation equally plausible.
- **Modal interchange:** borrowing from a parallel mode with the same tonic, supported by the chord tones and context. Name that mode, such as parallel natural minor, Dorian, or Mixolydian; do not call a relative-key relationship modal interchange.
- **Minor-key dominant:** a major V or V7 in minor commonly uses the raised leading tone from harmonic minor. This is not automatically modal interchange.
- **Tonicization versus modulation:** temporary focus does not establish a key change. Look for sustained local organization and cadential confirmation; report an insufficient excerpt honestly.
- **Cadence:** name a cadence only when phrase position, metric context, and actual chord motion support it. Root motion alone is not enough.

## Forward-Order Check

For an observed `F#m → C#7 → D → A`, retain that exact order.

An A-major context permits `vi → V7/vi → IV → I`, with C#7's G#–B–E# creating an applied-dominant pull toward F#m. The displayed sequence instead proceeds to D, so explain the avoided/delayed expected target and the actual D–A arrival. Do not claim C#7 resolves directly to the preceding F#m.

An F#-minor context permits `i → V7 → VI → III`. The V7–VI motion can be described as deceptive-like when appropriate, but a confirmed deceptive cadence requires phrase and metric evidence. C#7's raised leading tone E# is compatible with minor-key dominant practice; D alone does not prove modal borrowing.

These are contextual alternatives, not a fixed answer for every occurrence. Check the surrounding Moises progression before selecting the center and preserve uncertainty when only these four chords are available.

## Observation versus Interpretation

Keep the Moises symbol and analytical suggestion in separate fields or sentences. For instance, an uncertain displayed slash chord stays uncertain even if another reading seems functionally stronger. A chord list does not show exact voicings, individual-note resolution, melody, or phrase boundaries; do not describe those as observed.
