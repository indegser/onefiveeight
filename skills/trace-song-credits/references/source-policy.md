# Source and Credit Policy

## Resolve the Recording Before Its Credits

Treat a composition and a recording as different entities. Record at least the title, primary artist, release, release date or year, duration when available, version type, edition label, and stable service identifier. Keep separate identities for originals, remakes, covers, live recordings, single edits, album versions, remixes, remasters, re-recordings, and karaoke tracks.

Use a compilation appearance only as an access point to the same recording. Do not assume two equal-duration tracks are the same recording. Do not treat `Single Version`, `Radio Edit`, `Album Version`, or another explicit edition label as interchangeable until the underlying edit or master is verified. Prefer ISRC as strong corroboration when it is available, but do not require it.

## Rank Sources

Use the highest available tier for each claim:

1. Physical or digital liner notes; official artist, label, publisher, studio, or rights-society records.
2. Track-scoped credits from an established music service or distributor.
3. MusicBrainz, Discogs, or another curated discographic database with release-level provenance.
4. Direct interviews, reputable reporting, books, and specialist publications.
5. Wikis, blogs, fan pages, search snippets, and unsourced lyrics pages.

Use tier 5 only to discover stronger sources. If it remains the sole evidence, mark the claim `provisional` and do not build a strong recommendation on it.

## Attach Evidence to Claims

Store each credit as its own claim with:

- normalized person name,
- original displayed name,
- normalized role and original role text,
- track or release scope,
- source URL,
- source tier,
- confidence,
- recording version to which it applies.

Accept a claim as `high` confidence when one authoritative track-scoped source supports it. Require two independent credible sources when the source is indirect, the role is ambiguous, or another source conflicts.

## Preserve Conflicts

Do not vote away conflicts. Record both claims and explain whether they may describe different versions, role vocabularies, territories, or releases. Use `unresolved` when the evidence cannot adjudicate them.

Normalize cautiously:

- Do not translate `producer` into `arranger`.
- Do not translate `written by` into both music and lyrics unless the source defines it that way.
- Keep `string arranger`, `vocal arranger`, and `horn arranger` narrower than `arranger`.
- Keep performer and instrument information separate.
- Preserve aliases and romanization variants without merging possible namesakes.

## Prevent Common Leakage

- Never apply an album personnel list to every track automatically.
- Never carry remake performers back to the original recording.
- Never treat mastering or reissue staff as original production personnel.
- Never take credits from an adjacent track on the same page.
- Never use Spotify availability as proof of a songwriting or production credit.
- Never describe an influence relationship without a direct documentary source.

## Cite for the User

Place links near the claims they support. Prefer direct track, album-credit, label, publisher, or interview pages over search results. Paraphrase sources and keep quotations brief.
